AI in Lending: What Community Banks Are Actually Doing in 2026

9 min read By Marvin Tellez July 2, 2026 AI · Lending · Community Banking

Every major core provider, lending platform, and fintech vendor has an AI story right now. Some of them are real. Most of them are positioned several years ahead of where community institutions can actually deploy them.

After 26 years inside financial institutions — and the last decade specifically inside the Abrigo and Sageworks ecosystem — I've had a front-row seat to what actually gets implemented versus what gets demoed at conferences. The gap is significant.

This article is for CIOs, CLOs, and digital transformation leaders at community banks and credit unions who want to know what their peers are actually doing with AI in lending right now — not what the roadmap says, and not what the vendor promised.

68% of community banks report AI is a strategic priority for 2026
34% have deployed AI tools that are actively used in daily lending operations
3–5x faster financial statement spreading with AI-assisted tools vs. manual entry

Where AI Is Creating Real Value in Community Lending

Financial Statement Spreading

This is the single highest-adoption AI use case in community lending right now, and for good reason. Spreading financial statements manually is time-consuming, error-prone, and a poor use of your credit analysts' expertise. AI-assisted spreading tools — including capabilities now embedded in Abrigo — can reduce the time from hours to minutes on standard business financials.

The institutions getting the most out of this aren't just using it as a time-saver. They're using the freed analyst capacity to handle more complex credits and do deeper covenant review — which leads directly to the next use case.

Covenant Monitoring and Portfolio Surveillance

Machine learning components in portfolio monitoring tools can now flag deteriorating credits earlier than traditional periodic review cycles. Instead of catching a problem at the quarterly review, institutions using these tools are getting signals at the transaction or reporting period level — before the situation becomes a workout.

Abrigo's credit risk platform has been building toward this for several product cycles. Institutions that have both the platform and the data hygiene to support it are seeing real portfolio risk management benefits.

The institutions winning with AI in lending right now aren't the ones who moved fastest. They're the ones who got their data infrastructure right first — then let the tools do what they're actually good at.

Credit Risk Scoring Augmentation

Several community institutions are now using ML-augmented credit scoring as a secondary signal alongside traditional underwriting. This isn't replacing the credit officer's judgment — it's giving them a data point that traditional scorecards don't capture, particularly for small business credits where cash flow patterns tell a more complete story than the credit bureau alone.

This is further along in the credit union space than in community banking, largely because credit unions have historically been more willing to experiment with alternative data sources.

What Isn't Ready — Despite What You're Hearing

Autonomous Underwriting

Fully automated commercial underwriting — where the AI makes the credit decision without human review — is not something community institutions are deploying at scale. The regulatory exposure alone makes this a non-starter for most institutions under $10B. What you're seeing in pilots is AI as a workflow accelerator, not a decision-maker.

Conversational AI for Member and Customer Service

The large bank implementations you're reading about involve resources most community institutions don't have — dedicated AI teams, large proprietary data sets, and compliance infrastructure to handle the edge cases. Community banks deploying chatbots are mostly running third-party tools with limited customization, and member satisfaction scores have been mixed.

Cross-Sell Models That Outperform Relationship Managers

The premise is appealing: AI identifies the next best product for each customer. The reality is that cross-sell models require transaction data at a volume and depth that most community institutions don't yet have — and the models need continuous retraining to stay accurate. Your relationship managers are still outperforming the models at most institutions under $5B.

The Data Infrastructure Problem Nobody Talks About

The single biggest factor separating institutions that are getting value from AI from those that aren't is data quality — not budget, not vendor selection, not executive buy-in.

AI tools are only as good as the data you feed them. If your financial spreading data is inconsistent across analysts, if your core system has duplicate records, if your loan origination data isn't mapped cleanly to your credit risk platform — the AI will amplify those problems, not solve them.

Before evaluating any AI lending tool, the most important question to answer is: how clean is the underlying data it will be trained on or run against? This is a foundational infrastructure question, not a technology question.

What the Abrigo Ecosystem Looks Like in 2026

For institutions running Abrigo, the AI roadmap is more accessible than it might appear — but only if you've maximized the platform fundamentals first.

Spreading automation, covenant tracking, and portfolio risk signals are available now. The institutions using them effectively are the ones that went through proper implementation, built clean data workflows, and trained their credit teams on how to interpret the outputs alongside their own judgment.

The institutions that rushed implementation or deprioritized training are still using Abrigo primarily as a data repository — which means they're paying for a platform they're only using at 30–40% of its capacity. They have AI-ready tools sitting idle because the operational foundation wasn't built correctly underneath them.

Most institutions don't need a new platform. They need to actually use the one they already have — at the depth it was designed for.

A Practical Framework for 2026

If you're a community bank or credit union leader trying to cut through the AI noise and figure out where to actually invest your time and budget, here's the sequence that's working for institutions I work with:

  1. Audit your current platform utilization. Before evaluating new AI tools, understand what percentage of your current technology stack you're actually using. Most institutions are at 40–60% utilization on their core lending platforms.
  2. Fix the data before you feed it to AI. Data quality issues that are tolerable in manual processes become amplified when AI is involved. A data hygiene project is almost always the right first step.
  3. Start with high-volume, low-risk use cases. Spreading automation and portfolio monitoring are the right entry points. They're proven, they deliver measurable ROI, and they don't create regulatory exposure.
  4. Build staff fluency before expanding scope. AI tools fail when staff don't trust or understand the outputs. Training your credit team to interpret AI-generated signals alongside their own judgment is as important as the technology itself.
  5. Evaluate autonomous and predictive use cases in 12–18 months. The tooling is improving rapidly. The institutions that get their foundation right now will be positioned to deploy more advanced capabilities as the regulatory and technology landscape matures.
